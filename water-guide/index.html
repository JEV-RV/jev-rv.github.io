Section 1
Europe vs. North America — Water is Different

North American municipal water is typically heavily chlorinated at source and delivered through modern, regularly inspected infrastructure. In Europe, standards vary dramatically by country, region, and even campsite. Understanding what you're dealing with is the first step.

🇪🇺

European Municipal Water

Generally safe to drink by EU standards, but hardness (CaCO₃) varies from 50 mg/L (soft, Atlantic arc) to 400+ mg/L (hard, central Europe). Lower chlorine residual than North America — bacteria can survive in low-flow campsite lines. pH ranges from 6.5 to 8.5 depending on source.

🇨🇦 🇺🇸

North American Municipal Water

Standardized chlorination at 0.2–4 mg/L free chlorine residual throughout distribution. Stricter turbidity limits (<0.3 NTU at treatment). Your RV was designed around this profile — its seals, membranes, and tanks were tested with this type of water.

⛺

Campsite & Rural Sources

Campsite taps often have long, low-flow lines with seasonal stagnation. Rural well water can contain iron, manganese, or biological contamination. Boondocking sources (rivers, cisterns) are untested. Turbidity >1 NTU reduces UV and chlorine effectiveness significantly (EPA threshold).

🦠

Legionella Risk

Legionella optimally grows at 32–35°C and survives at 20–45°C (CDC 2025, WHO 2022). A parked RV in summer Europe, with stagnant water in tanks and hoses warmed by sun, is a near-ideal environment. Risk increases sharply after >7 days stagnation above 20°C.

Section 2
The 20% Tank Rule in Transit

One of the most effective practices for keeping your fresh tank clean during European travel has nothing to do with chemistry.

📏

Why Keep the Tank Low?

Travelling with >80% water creates sloshing that generates biofilm-detaching turbulence, potentially resuspending contaminants. More importantly, a near-empty tank dries faster after flushing, reducing bacterial colonization. The "20% rule" — keep ≤20% during driving — minimizes weight, slosh, and stagnation surface area.

🗓️

Stagnation Window

At summer European temperatures (25–35°C), Legionella and coliforms can reach concerning levels within 3–7 days in a partially filled stagnant tank. Refill from a fresh source every 2–3 days when parked in heat. In cool weather (<15°C), risk is much lower — Legionella is inactive below 20°C.

💡

Practical Strategy

Fill to 100% only at verified quality sources before a static stay. During driving days, carry only what you need that day. At new campsites, flush the campsite tap for 30–60 seconds before connecting. Never connect directly to a hose that's been baking in the sun for hours.

Barrier 1 — BLUTEC AR3 at Inlet

Campsite-tap pre-filter (5µm sediment + 0.2µm ceramic + UV-LED). Removes particulates and inactivates bacteria before water enters the tank. Only usable at pressurized campsite connections — not compatible with boondocking bucket fill.

⚡

Barrier 2 — Ozone Shock Treatment (Optional)

Airthereal AH1000 (1000 mg/h) injected into tank for 45–75 min depending on protocol. Destroys bacteria, viruses, and biofilm. Ozone reverts to O₂ — no residue. Carbon #1 neutralizes any residual before the pump. NEVER combined with Aquatabs in same cycle.

💎

Barrier 3 — Reverse Osmosis (NOVO CRO-800UX6)

Tankless RO at point-of-use (kitchen). Removes dissolved solids, heavy metals, nitrates, and any residual chemical contamination. Protects from hard water scaling at the most critical consumption point.

🔆

Barrier 4 — UV LED (ACUVA 1.2)

Final UV sterilization post-RO. Pure RO water (TDS <50) is ideal for UV — zero turbidity means maximum photon penetration efficiency. Positioned after RO and before remineralization: treats the most vulnerable stage of the water path.

Installation Guide — Unity vs. Wonder

The LTV Unity (Sprinter chassis) and Wonder (Transit chassis) have different plumbing architectures. The Unity already has an inline carbon filter; the Wonder does not. Installation scenarios also differ between campsite (pressurized) and boondocking (tank fill).

The Unity has a Sprinter inline carbon filter (part of the stock plumbing). The Skøl system adds BLUTEC at the inlet, ozone in the tank, and a NOVO RO+ACUVA+remineralizer at the kitchen point-of-use.

→ Fresh Tank (97L) [O₃ optional: 45–75 min]
→ Sprinter Carbon [factory inline — neutralizes O₃ residual]
→ Shurflo Pump ⚠ NEVER run during O₃
→ Carbon #2 [pre-RO safety filter]
→ NOVO CRO-800UX6 RO [tankless, kitchen]
→ ACUVA 1.2 UV-LED [post-RO sterilization]
→ Waterdrop ALK35 [remineralization + pH↑]

BOONDOCKING MODE (bucket fill)
Water source → BLUTEC NOT USABLE (no pressure)

Key note: The Sprinter factory carbon filter counts as Carbon #1 in the Skøl architecture. Do not remove it — it is essential for O₃ neutralization before the pump.

The Wonder (Transit chassis) does not include a factory inline carbon filter. You must add Carbon #1 manually between tank output and pump.

🔧 Tank Vent Verification Procedure

▼

The tank vent must be functional for ozone treatment to work correctly. If sealed, O₃ pressure buildup risks damaging fittings. Verify before first ozone use:

  1. Overfill method: Fill tank completely until water drains from the vent outlet underneath the RV. Confirms vent is open and draining correctly.
  2. Soapy water test: Apply soapy water to all tank fittings while O₃ generator is running. No bubbles = no leaks.
  3. Visual inspection: Check vent screen for wasp nests, debris, or ice plug (if stored in cold).
  4. After vent confirmation: Mark date on tank maintenance log. Repeat annually or after winterization.

Note: This vent procedure is specific to the ozone context. No other LTV community member currently uses ozone — there is no established precedent to reference.

Section 7
Complete Protocol Reference

Detailed step-by-step for each ozone protocol. Click to expand.

🟢 STD — Standard Protocol (Municipal, T<25°C, <7 days)

▼
  • Pre-conditions: Verified municipal water, <7 days stagnation, temperature <25°C
  • Ozone duration: 30–45 min if T<15°C / 45–60 min if 15–25°C
  • Timer note: Airthereal AH1000 max timer = 60 min. Use single cycle for STD.
  • Pump safety wait: 30–60 min after O₃ stops (2× half-life at 15°C ≈ 60 min)
  • Purge: Light purge 1–2 min at each tap before use
  • Max frequency: Once per month recommended

🟡 VAR — Variable Risk Protocol (Well / Unknown, 3–14 days)

▼
  • Pre-conditions: Well, rural, or unknown source; OR municipal water with 3–14 days stagnation
  • Ozone duration: 45–60 min
  • Pump safety wait: 45–90 min (2–3× half-life depending on T)
  • Purge: Partial purge — run all taps 2–3 min
  • Max frequency: Maximum 2× per month

🔴 CHOC — Shock Protocol (T>25°C, >7 days stagnation, hot boondocking)

▼
  • Pre-conditions: T>25°C AND stagnation >7 days, OR summer boondocking in hot climate
  • Ozone duration: 60–75 min MAX (EPDM budget concern above 75 min)
  • Pump safety wait: 30 min minimum after O₃ stops, then complete drain + rinse + refill with fresh source
  • Complete drain required: Do not reuse the treated water. Drain, rinse tank, refill from clean source.
  • Max frequency: Sparingly — budget impact high

⚪ 2×VOL — Double Volumetric (Full 97L tank with 60 min timer limit)

▼

Purpose: This is NOT a biological protocol — it is a logistics solution. The Airthereal AH1000 has a maximum 60-minute electronic timer. For a full 97L tank, one 60-min cycle may not achieve sufficient O₃ contact. The double volumetric protocol covers the full tank.

  1. Run O₃ generator for 60 min (timer limit)
  2. Pause 15–20 min to allow timer reset
  3. Run second 60 min cycle
  4. Wait pump safety time per STD protocol

⚠ This is 2×60 = 120 min of ozone — significant EPDM budget impact. Use maximum once per month.

🔴 2×BIO — Double Biological (Forest / Mold / >14 days stagnation)

▼

Purpose: Biological protocol targeting spore-forming organisms. Spores can survive O₃ in dry form — the rest period allows rehydration, making them vulnerable to the second cycle.

  1. Cycle 1: Run O₃ 60–75 min
  2. Rest 1–2 hours — spores rehydrate
  3. Complete drain of tank
  4. Refill with fresh clean water
  5. Cycle 2: Run O₃ 45 min
  6. Full rest period, then purge all taps

⚠ Use only when biological contamination is suspected (forest source, standing water, visible discoloration or odor). High EPDM budget impact.

🚫 NEVER Combine O₃ and Aquatabs

Mixing ozone and chlorine (Aquatabs = NaDCC) in the same water produces chlorates and chlorites — disinfection byproducts that are potentially harmful. Always ensure complete water renewal (drain + refill) between ozone and chlorine treatments. These two methods are mutually exclusive in the same treatment cycle.

Resources & Verification
🗺️

EU Water Quality Database

Check local municipality water quality reports. Most EU countries publish annual reports at the commune level. Search "[commune name] qualité eau potable" (France) or "[municipality] drinking water quality" (NL/BE/DE).

📍

Park4Night — Potable Water Filter

Use the Eau potable filter in Park4Night to find verified potable water points near your route. Community-validated — not a substitute for treatment, but helps avoid obviously suspect sources.

🔬

Local Lab Testing

In France: Laboratoires départementaux d'analyse (LDA) offer field water testing for €30–80. In Spain: LABAQUA. In Portugal: Laboratório Nacional de Engenharia Civil (LNEC). Test for: coliforms, Legionella, nitrates, turbidity.

🌡️

Water Temperature Monitor

A Tuya-compatible DS18B20 waterproof probe (≈€15) placed in the tank gives real-time temperature data on your phone. Recommended for summer travel — temperature is the single most important variable for protocol selection.
